Due 1 · Quiet — the minimum that still reads. An undue task keeps its row but drops to --fg-faint with a dimmed circle, under a hairline labelled Not due yet. Coming due does two things only: the text returns to full white and the glyph goes from ⏳ to ⏰ (🔁 when it repeats). No pills, no bars, no colour.

The bet: on a list you scan daily, a row going from grey to white is already a change you notice, and the app has no other loud state to compete with. If that turns out too subtle to catch after two weeks away, Due 2 is the answer.

Glyph slot is free: it's the same trailing position the Claude section already uses for its send arrow, and the two never collide — a Claude row is a receipt with no schedule, a scheduled row is never in the Claude section.

Cost of the schedule: Item has no time fields, so this is a 6 → 7 migration — dueAt INTEGER and repeatWeeks INTEGER, both nullable, no backfill. Back up .db + -wal + -shm first; fallbackToDestructiveMigration is off on purpose. Ordering needed no SQL change: observeBySection and listBySection stay byte-identical and the split happens in Kotlin, so the widget can't drift from the app. (Built: an earlier draft of this caption called for a leading sort key with a :now parameter — that would have frozen the Flow at the instant it was created.)

Two definitions of "open": the rail badge comes from observeOpenCounts, but the widget counts its own caption locally in ClassicWidgetRenderer. Excluding undue rows means editing both. The header above says 3 open, not 5.

Nothing in this app runs on a timer. Due-ness is evaluated when the list is composed, so the app is right every time you open it — but the widget has updatePeriodMillis="0" and only redraws on a write, so a task that comes due overnight won't surface there until something else happens. Re-rendering on app resume covers the realistic case; a punctual widget needs AlarmManager.
